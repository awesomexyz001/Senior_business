## 1. How big is the Japanese 見守りサービス market, and how did it grow 2020–2026?

### Takeaway
Every public estimate is small. The elderly 見守り/emergency-call market is roughly ¥10–25 billion (about 100–230億円) a year, against a population of 36 million+ seniors. The latest free numbers are pre-2021 forecasts. The 2020–2026 actuals sit inside paid reports (矢野経済研究所, 富士経済) that this session could not open. Forecasts made in 2015–2020 expected the market to roughly double by 2025.

### Cited Findings
- 富士経済 (2018 release): the 見守り segment of the elderly/care products and services market was estimated at **¥7.5B (75億円) in 2018**, with a forecast of **¥12.4B (124億円) in 2025**, or 193.8% of 2017. [snippet] — [財経新聞 2018-07-28](https://www.zaikei.co.jp/article/20180728/456276.html); [日刊工業新聞 「見守り」市場、25年に2倍](https://www.nikkan.co.jp/articles/view/00483033)
- 富士経済 (2020 release, "介護関連製品・サービス市場は1.1兆円(2030年予測)"): 見守り関連 was **¥10.7B (107億円) in 2020 (estimate)**, forecast to reach **¥21.2B (212億円)**, or 2.3x, by the forecast year, apparently 2030. The snippet does not state the year clearly. [snippet] — [富士経済 press cid=21030](https://www.fuji-keizai.co.jp/press/detail.html?cid=21030&la=ja&la=en)
- シード・プランニング (c.2015, cited by TKC): the elderly 見守り and emergency-call (緊急通報) service market was **¥14.2B in 2014**, expected to reach **¥22.7B in 2025**. [snippet] — [TKC 参入企業続々！見守りビジネス (2015-05)](https://www.tkc.jp/cc/senkei/201505_special01/)
- 矢野経済研究所 publishes 「介護ICT・高齢者向けICTサービス市場の将来展望」, which covers the 個人向け見守り market for elderly people living alone. The newest edition found is the 2021 edition. The press release lists a chart titled 「高齢者向けICT(個人向け見守り)サービス(金額)の実績及び予測」, but its figures could not be retrieved because the site was blocked and the report is paid. The 2025 edition of 「シニア関連市場マーケティング年鑑」 also includes a 見守りサービス section. [snippet] — [Yano 2021 report page](https://www.yano.co.jp/market_reports/C63118800); [Yano press 3637](https://www.yano.co.jp/press-release/more/press_id/3637); [Yano 2025 シニア年鑑](https://www.yano.co.jp/market_reports/C67109300)
- Comparison point: 富士キメラ総研 (2025) forecasts the **children's school-commute (登下校) 見守り market at ¥28.1B in FY2030**, 2.9x FY2023. That makes the child segment about as large as, or larger than, the elderly segment. [snippet] — [富士経済グループ広報 on X](https://x.com/FkgKoho/status/1904752674454819093)

### Inferences
- Even the optimistic forecasts (¥12–23B by 2025) imply only a few hundred thousand paying households at ¥2,000–5,000/month. Penetration is in the low single digits, which matches the 2–5% usage figures in the surveys in section 3.
- Market definitions differ: some count security-company emergency call, some count ICT/sensor services only. Do not add these numbers together.

### Gaps
- No 2022–2026 actual market-size figures were obtained. 矢野経済研究所 and シード・プランニング pages were blocked or paywalled. A follow-up should allow yano.co.jp and fuji-keizai.co.jp, or buy the reports.

## 2. Case studies with metrics

### Takeaway
The longest-running family-paid Japanese 見守り products reach only about 10,000–25,000 subscribers after 10–25 years. Examples: 象印 about 15,000 cumulative over 25 years; 日本郵便 peaked near 24,000 and then lost 38%; ハローライト about 9,000–16,000. The services that scaled are either free or near-free (エンリッチ LINE: 23,000 registrations free; 関西電力: free until 2027-03), or bundled into security, telecom or municipal channels. Pricing has been moving **up** for physical-visit services: ヤマト raised its price 61% in 2025.

### Cited Findings

The **contract holder is the watching family member**, who orders through 象印ダイレクト. The kettle is rented, with no device charge. **Initial fee ¥5,500, monthly ¥3,300 (tax incl.)**, first month free trial. [snippet] — [MONOist 2023-05](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/spv/2305/25/news126.html); [INTERNET Watch](https://internet.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1485779.html); [象印ダイレクト](https://www.zojirushi-direct.com/ext/mimamori.html)
- 2023 renewal: alerts for boil-dry and for **non-use** were added, so the service now detects an absence of activity as well as activity. [snippet] — [日経 2023-03](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUF144AH0U3A310C2000000/)

### Inferences
- **Ceiling signal:** 象印 is the best-known brand in the category, with 25 years and strong media coverage, and still has only about 15,000 **cumulative** contracts. Family-paid, single-device 見守り is a niche of thousands to tens of thousands, not millions.
- **Sales-push risk:** 日本郵便's 38% collapse shows that headline subscriber numbers driven by a captive salesforce can be hollow. Real demand was much lower than the 24,000 peak.
- **Price direction:** human-visit costs are rising. Yamato's +61% increase is likely a reflection of driver labor shortages, though that is not confirmed by source. Pure-data services are converging on ¥100–550/month (utilities, carriers). A Korean software-only entrant would be priced against the low band unless it bundles human follow-up.
- **Mechanism pattern:** passive sensing of something the parent already does daily (kettle, light bulb, electricity, TV) is the dominant Japanese design. It lowers 監視感 and needs no new behavior from the parent.

## 3. Why is uptake low, and what triggers purchase?

### Takeaway
About half of caregiving families say they are interested, but only **about 2–5% actually use** a 見守り service. Price is the number one decision factor (72%). The most common actual spend is **¥0–300/month**. People who quit cite not understanding the service (31%) and cost (28%). The parent's psychological burden (監視感) is the top selection criterion, so passive sensors are favored over cameras.

### Cited Findings
- 安心介護 survey (エス・エム・エス, 2023-07-20, 500 caregiving family members): **about half interested, but only 5% had used a service**. The most-used type was emergency-call, followed by camera and sensor. Among users, the most common monthly cost was **¥0–300 (24.1%)**. [snippet] — [bm-sms press 2023-07-20](https://www.bm-sms.co.jp/news-press/prs_20230720_ansinkaigo_research/); [PR TIMES](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000298.000013298.html)
- マイボイスコム survey: **about 2% currently use** a 見守り service and just under 30% intend to. Among those who intend to, **"料金" (price) is the top criterion at 72.2%**. Reasons for not using or quitting include **"よくわからない" 31.0%** and **"金銭的負担" 28.2%**. The services used most were periodic in-person visits and emergency buttons, each about 20%. [snippet] — [MyVoice](https://www.myvoice.co.jp/biz/surveys/24805/index.html); [PR TIMES MyVoice](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000001246.000007815.html)
- 介護ポストセブン: about 5% have used one, and about 20% of those rely on informal checks such as newspaper or meal delivery. [snippet] — [介護ポストセブン](https://kaigo-postseven.com/199427)
- 東京ガス 都市生活研究所 survey of parents and children: the **number one selection criterion is "親の心理的負担が少ない"**, i.e. low psychological burden on the parent. Cameras carry the highest 監視 stress; sensors carry less because there is no image. [snippet] — [東京ガス ウチコト](https://uchi.tokyo-gas.co.jp/topics/6224)
- Privacy and 監視 concerns are also raised as the main drawback in landlord-facing guides. [snippet] — [owners-cb](https://owners-cb.jp/topics/7859)
- Price bands per landlord guides:
  - Emergency-call: ¥3,000–4,000/month
  - Phone dialogue: ¥1,000–3,000/month
  - Sensor: from about ¥2,000/month
  [snippet] — [sumaity owners](https://owners.sumaity.com/cat_item/press_553/)
- Trigger evidence: エンリッチ was founded after a family 孤独死. 日本郵便 users average 83.2 years old, which suggests purchase happens late, at frailty, rather than at the start of living alone. [snippet] — [LIFULL](https://kaigo.homes.co.jp/tayorini/report/enrich/); [日本郵便 2023](https://www.post.japanpost.jp/notification/productinformation/2023/0724_01.html)

### Inferences
- The willingness-to-pay anchor is very low, at hundreds of yen. Free municipal and utility offers likely anchor expectations further. A Korean product at a few thousand won per month would match this anchor; ₩30,000+ would need a human-response component.
- Purchase is event-driven and late, around age 80+ or after a scare. Marketing aimed at "adult children at the moment of a trigger" (hospital discharge, a fall, a parent being widowed) is plausible, but trigger statistics were not found.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of purchase triggers (退院, 一人暮らし開始, 孤独死 news) was found.
- No churn-reason data from the providers themselves was found.

## 4. B2B2C: landlords, insurers and municipalities as payers

### Takeaway
In Japan the institutional payer is often the stronger channel. Landlords buy 孤独死保険 (a few hundred yen per unit per month) and bulk 見守り to make renting to single elderly tenants acceptable. Insurers such as Aioi Nissay Dowa sell municipality-paid policies. Municipalities subsidize devices and monthly fees (豊中, 文京, 厚木, the 22 local governments using 日本郵便 smart speakers). Family-paid D2C is the weakest channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Aioi Nissay Dowa (2022-11-28)** launched what it calls Japan's first **municipality-purchased insurance covering landlords' 孤独死-related costs**, modeled on 名古屋市's scheme, to encourage landlords to accept elderly tenants. [snippet] — [あいおいニッセイ同和 PDF](https://www.aioinissaydowa.co.jp/corporate/about/news/pdf/2022/news_2022112801083.pdf)
- Landlord-type 孤独死保険: the landlord is the policyholder and pays premiums, sometimes folded into rent. It covers restoration and lost rent at **a few hundred yen per unit per month**. The average 孤独死 loss is cited at about ¥630,000. Some municipalities subsidize 見守り device and monthly costs. [snippet] — [landnet](https://rent.landnet.co.jp/topics/3927/); [minikuruhome 岡山](https://minikuruhome.co.jp/okayama-ooyasan-kodokushi-mimamori-hoken/)
- **大家さん安心コール**: a 見守り call service sold only to real-estate agents, with no initial fee and monthly billing per watched tenant. [snippet] — [ooyacall.jp](https://ooyacall.jp/)
- Real-estate industry bodies distribute the 日本郵便 みまもり flyer to members (宅建協会). [snippet] — [htk.or.jp](https://www.htk.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/mimamori.pdf)
- 国交省 materials list 見守り and new technology as part of housing-industry policy for elderly tenants. [snippet] — [MLIT 住生活関連産業](https://www.mlit.go.jp/policy/shingikai/content/001385613.pdf)

### Inferences
- The landlord's problem (vacancy loss and cleanup cost after an undiscovered death) is concrete and quantifiable at about ¥630k. That makes the per-unit ROI easier to sell than to an adult child's diffuse anxiety.

### Gaps
- R65不動産's specific model and pricing were not found.
- No figures were found on how many rental units carry 見守り.
- The 2024–2025 居住支援 law changes (改正住宅セーフティネット法, 居住サポート住宅) were not researched in this session.

## 5. How well do these lessons transfer to Korea?

### Takeaway
Korea already has large **free, state-funded** coverage:
- 응급안전안심서비스: about 277,000 households in 2025, targeted to reach 300,000
- Naver 클로바 케어콜 (AI calls bought by local governments): about 30,000 users (2024-08), reported about 60,000 by 2026
- 한전 1인가구 안부살핌 (electricity data, with LG U+/SKT telecom data): about 13,000 people in 88 local governments

A paid family product in Korea would therefore compete against free government services, much as Japan's paid services compete against municipal offers. The gap is non-low-income parents who are not eligible for the government programs and families who want richer and faster information.

### Cited Findings
- **응급안전안심서비스 (MOHW)**: in-home fire and activity sensors plus emergency buttons. **About 168,000 households (2022) rising to about 277,000 (2025)**, with about 300,000 targeted by 2026. Eligibility was expanded to two-elder households and grandparent-grandchild households. In 2025 the service handled **385,033 responses**: 25,050 emergency calls, 8,111 fire alerts and **351,872 safety checks triggered by no detected activity**. [snippet] — [세계일보 2025-09-02](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20250902515670); [korea.kr](https://m.korea.kr/briefing/pressReleaseView.do?newsId=156580943)

### Inferences
- **Korea is ahead of Japan on public AI-call and electricity-data 안부 coverage.** Korea has 60k AI-call users against no comparable Japanese public scheme, and 277k sensor households against Japan's patchwork of municipal programs. Both are free to the user and means-tested or targeted. The open space is **middle-income parents whose adult children pay**, which matches the Japanese D2C segment but should be expected to be small: tens of thousands, not hundreds of thousands, per Japanese precedent.
- The Japanese evidence suggests these design rules for a small Korean software team:
  1. Use passive, no-new-behavior signals (phone usage, smart plug, electricity) rather than cameras.
  2. Price near ₩3,000–10,000 per month, matching the Japanese ¥300–1,800 band, with paid human escalation.
  3. Pursue a B2B2C or B2G channel (local governments, landlords, 보험사, 통신사) alongside D2C, because Japan's D2C leaders stayed at about 15k–25k subscribers.
  4. Position on "connection" (photos, chat with grandchildren, like まごチャンネル) rather than "monitoring" to reduce parent resistance.
- Japan Post's collapse shows the risk of relying on a partner's salesforce. Watch churn closely after any bundle push.
