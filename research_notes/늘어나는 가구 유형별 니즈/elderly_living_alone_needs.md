## 1. Segment size, trend and projections (2024–2026 → 2052)

### Takeaway
About 2.45–2.48 million Korean households are 65+ people living alone in 2025–2026. That is 38% of all older-headed households and about 11% of all households. The 2022-base projection roughly doubles this to 4.96 million by 2052, when 42.1% of older households will be single-person. Women dominate the segment: 43.2% of women aged 65+ live alone, against 19.6% of men. The share living alone rises steeply after 75 (52–54% at 85+).

### Inferences
- Expect roughly 2.5M households in 2026, about 3.0M by 2030 and about 4.2M by 2040. The fastest-growing part is 75+ women living alone, the group most limited in function and most digitally excluded (see §2). The 65–74 group living alone is smaller per cohort but includes the growing number of divorced or never-married men.
- When sizing, use households (2.45–2.48M) for home services and safety devices. Use persons (about 2.2M, 22%) only when the persons basis is explicitly needed.

## 2. Needs evidence: ranked list of concrete needs (2023 노인실태조사 독거 vs couple households, plus other sources)

### Takeaway
Need is highest for **someone to care for me when I'm sick and help with daily tasks**. 39.9% of older people living alone name "아플 때 간호" as their main difficulty, and 68.4% say they need daily-living support at home. Next come **safety/emergency response**, **home-visit health care**, **escort to hospital and on outings**, **home repair**, **meals and nutrition**, **money**, **loneliness and depression**, **digital help** and **end-of-life arrangements**. On nearly every indicator, older people living alone are worse off than older couples: health, depression, nutrition, frailty, income, digital access and having enough care.

### Cited Findings: headline gaps (2023, 독거 vs 노인부부) [V] — [press release](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/pressReleaseView.do?newsId=156655081) + [full report](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10411010100&bid=0019&act=view&list_no=1483359)
- Self-rated "healthy": 34.2% vs 48.6%. Depressive symptoms: **16.1% vs 7.8%**. Average chronic diseases: 2.4 vs 2.0. Nutrition "good": **54.4% vs 71.2%**. "Has difficulties living alone/as a single household": **73.9% vs 48.1%**.
- Frailty (표 4-7): frail 7.1% / pre-frail 38.3% / robust 54.6% for those living alone, vs 2.8% / 27.6% / 69.6% for couples.
- Persons who would have nobody to help them (all 65+): 6.6% overall, rising with age from 4.6% (65–69) to 12.9% (85+).

### Ranked needs with evidence

**#1 Care when sick and daily-living help (가사·일상생활 지원, 간병)**

Couples: no difficulty 51.9%, care when sick 16.5%. For people aged 90+, care when sick reaches 40.3%. Rural respondents (all single-generation households) report care when sick at 29.4% and daily matters at 12.5%. Lower income and lower education concentrate the difficulty in care when sick and daily matters. [V; two columns I]
- Need for home-based services (표 10-23~28; % saying they need it / % willing to pay, living alone): **daily-living support 68.4% / 52.5%** · home-visit medical/health 62.6% / 51.8% · safety support 62.0% / 41.8% · hospital/outing escort 61.3% / 47.6% · home-environment improvement 56.8% / 39.2% · counselling 43.5% / 29.5%. Willingness to pay is consistently lower among those living alone than among couples (e.g., daily support: 52.5% vs 59.1%). [V]
- Among those living alone **with functional limitation**, only 53.3% receive any care (n=390). Care providers (multiple answers): non-co-resident family 63.1% · long-term care insurance (LTCI) 40.1% · relatives/neighbours/friends 28.3% · private carer/housekeeper 14.4% · **노인맞춤돌봄 only 7.3%** · other public 9.1%. [V — 표 4-19]
- Care sufficiency for those living alone and receiving care (표 4-28, n=374): insufficient 25.0% + very insufficient 1.8% = **26.8%**, vs 12.3% for couples and 18.3% overall. [V]
- Why people living alone with limitations get no help (표 4-29, n=341): don't need it 45.7% · **don't want to burden family 27.7%** · nobody to help 17.1% · cost 4.8% · dislike strangers coming in 1.5% · don't know how 3.2%. [V for first value; I for column order of the rest]
- What older people want improved in care services, 1+2 ranks combined (표 4-33, living alone): more service types 52.5% · longer hours 52.0% · rehabilitation/function training 51.9% · weekend/night availability 21.6% · short-term post-discharge care 13.1%. [V]

**#2 Safety, emergencies and check-in (안전·안부 확인)**

- 응급안전안심서비스 responses in 2025: emergency calls 25,050 · fire detections 8,111 · **no-activity safety checks 351,872** · total 385,033 incidents for about 300,000 households living alone. [V] — [MOHW press release (dated "2025. 4. 1.(수)" in the PDF; the minister named and the "2025 기준" annual data imply it was actually released 2026-04-01)](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1489949)

- Falls: 6.8% of those living alone fell in the past year (표 4-8). Safety items are covered in table 10-10. [V for the 6.8% figure]

**#3 Health, chronic disease and medication**
- Need for home-visit medical/health services: 62.6% (표 10-26). [V]
- Daily prescription drugs (표 3-7, living alone): none 13.3% · 1–2 drugs 49.0% · 3–5 drugs 31.7% · 6+ drugs 6.0% [I bins]. Average chronic diseases: 2.4. [V average; bins I]
- Health-care improvement priorities, rank 1 (표 3-22, living alone): regular health check-up support 33.4% · health-promotion education/counselling 25.7% · medical-cost support 26.5% · services in medically underserved areas 7.2% · home visits 6.3%. [I column order]
- Depression 16.1% (vs 7.8% for couples). Suicidal ideation in the past year: 2.0% of those living alone vs 0.5% of couples. Among those with ideation (n=65, small), **loneliness is the reason for 28.3%**, vs 23.3% overall and 15.8% for couples; physical illness is the reason for 46.7%. [V — 표 3-5, 3-6]
- National: medical spending per person aged 65+ was 537.6만원 in 2024, of which 125.6만원 was out of pocket. Health check-up rate falls with age: 79.0% (65–69), 73.8% (70–79), **43.8% (80+)** in 2024. [V] — [2026 고령자 통계](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)
- Seoul home-health register (about 14,000 older people): 74% are frail or pre-frail. People living alone are about 1.2 times more likely to be frail. Frail people are more than 3 times as likely not to go out even once a week. [S] — [노컷뉴스](https://www.nocutnews.co.kr/news/6444934)

**#7 Loneliness, emotional support and social contact**
- Loneliness or psychological anxiety is the main difficulty for 11.6% of those living alone (vs 4.2% for couples). [V]
- People to turn to when depressed (표 5-3): none 9.4% for those living alone (couples 7.0%), average 2.4 people. When sick and needing housework help (표 5-4): none 16.1% (couples 13.5%). [V]

### Differences by segment (all 2023 survey unless noted)
- **Age 65–74 vs 75+:** functional and digital needs rise steeply with age. Internet use is 95.0% at 65–69 vs 55.3% at 80+ (2025). Smartphone ownership is 94.9% at 65–69 vs 55.0% at 80–84 (2023). Health check-up rate is 43.8% at 80+. "Nobody to help" goes from 4.6% at 65–69 to 12.9% at 85+. Interest in hobbies: 49.2% of 65–69 are willing (7.4+41.8) vs 28.5% of 80–84. The top reason for not taking part in leisure shifts from "no time" (35.3% at 65–69) to "don't know how" (31.1% at 80–84). [V]
- **Sex:** women make up most of those living alone. Women are more likely to report sickness-care difficulty (30.2% vs 19.2%, all single-generation households, 표 5-9) and outing barriers (only 37.0% report none vs 51.5% for men). Men more often say "I don't need help" (66.0% vs 45.5%, 표 4-29) and use the 경로당 less. Men's support networks are shrinking (Social Survey 2015→2025, −4.8%p). Men dominate 고독사 (81.7%). [V]
- **Urban vs rural:** rural older people live alone slightly more (35.3% vs 31.9%). Rural single households most often chose to live alone because they did not want to leave their place (31.1%); urban ones because their children married (26.9%). Rural: 경로당 use 48.6% (all rural 65+), friends seen 4+ times a week 25.8%, lack of transport 20.2%, home internet 35.1%. Rural people feel more of the "care when sick" difficulty (29.4%). [V]
- **Income:** lower income goes with more difficulty (concentrated in sickness care and daily matters) and with staying put "because I don't want to leave". Higher-income older people choose to live alone for independence or couple life. 12.1% of those living alone receive basic livelihood benefits. [V — report text on 표 5-8/5-9]

### Inferences
- The core unmet need is a **reliable person for the "between" moments**: a sudden illness, a hospital discharge, a broken appliance, a confusing digital task. Routine monitoring is not the gap. Families are the default helper (63% for those living alone with limitations), but older people hold back because they "don't want to be a burden" (27.7%).
- Willingness to pay among those living alone is about 40–53% depending on the service, lower than among couples. That is consistent with their much lower income (2,086만원 vs 3,469만원). Paid models probably fit adult children paying, or the higher-income non-기초연금 group.
- Nutrition is a clear gap (54% "good") while only about 6.5% use meal delivery. Meals are also a main reason those living alone visit the 경로당, which points to demand for **shared meals** as much as for delivered food.

### Gaps
- The 2023 survey has no single question asking what service people living alone need most. The closest are the home-service needs table (10-23~28) and the policy-priority table (10-61). Tables marked [I] should be checked against the HWPX or Excel original before quoting precisely.
- No 2023 cross-tabulation of 독거 × sex × age (e.g., men 75+ living alone) was found in the published tables; it would need the microdata (data.kihasa.re.kr).
- No national data found on **home-repair requests** (e.g., 집수리 demand counts) specific to those living alone.

## 3. What public services cover, and where coverage stops

### Takeaway
The public safety net for people living alone has three layers. **노인맞춤돌봄** (about 550,000 users, means-tested at the 기초연금 line) gives in-person check-ins, housework and escort. **응급안전안심** (about 300,000 households living alone, no income test since 2024) gives ICT sensors and 119 links. **AI 안부콜** and local programmes add a light check-in layer. Coverage stops at: (a) the roughly 30% of older people above the 기초연금 line, meaning the middle and upper-middle income groups; (b) hours (about 16–20 hours a month, weekday daytime only); (c) people living alone already on LTCI, who are excluded; (d) men who refuse help; (e) people needing more variety, more time, rehabilitation and night or weekend cover.

### Cited Findings
- **노인맞춤돌봄 scale:** 550,000 users. About 60,000 of them are in the 중점돌봄군 (severe mobility limits), whose hours rose from about 16 to 20+ a month from Jan 2024. Staff: about 36,000 social workers and care aides plus 2,400 added. Proxy applications are now explicitly allowed from welfare centres (수행기관). [V] — [MOHW 2024-01-04](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1479665)
- **노인맞춤돌봄 eligibility:** aged 65+ **and** a 기초생활수급자, 차상위계층 or **기초연금 recipient**, living alone, in a grandparent-grandchild household or in an older-couple household. [V] — [MOHW 2026-07-22](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1491373). LTCI grade holders must in principle give up LTCI to use it; those whose grade has expired can apply. [S, blog] — [korea.legal](https://korea.legal/wiki/%eb%b3%b4%ea%b1%b4%eb%b3%b5%ec%a7%80%eb%b6%80-2026%eb%85%84-%eb%85%b8%ec%9d%b8%eb%a7%9e%ec%b6%a4%eb%8f%8c%eb%b4%84-%eb%8c%80%ec%83%81-%ec%8b%a0%ec%b2%ad-%ea%b2%b0%ec%a0%95%ea%b3%bc/)
- **New in 2026, post-discharge short-term intensive service (퇴원환자 단기집중서비스):** started March 2026 alongside 통합돌봄. It provides nutrition, housework and escort for 1 month (44 hours, extendable by 1 month) through 276 hub agencies. 1,392 users as of 2026-07-17. From 2026-07-27 an emergency track delivers service within 3–7 days of applying (apply within 14 days of discharge), replacing a process that took up to 1 month or more. [V] — [MOHW 2026-07-22](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1491373)
- 2026 (blog sources): the special service for depression and 고독사 risk reportedly no longer requires a depression diagnosis. [S, low reliability] — [tilnote](https://tilnote.io/pages/6a9e24e78a847deea1798eb3)
- **응급안전안심서비스:** income criterion for people living alone abolished in 2024, so "any older person living alone can apply". Non-eligible households can use it at their own cost. Equipment: fire detector, emergency call button (including voice), radar activity sensor (movement, heart rate, breathing), door sensor and gateway. Coverage: 164,796 older households in 2022 and **227,328 in 2023**. [V] — [MOHW 2024-04-04](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1480948). About **300,000 households living alone** were covered by 2025–26, with a 5th project (2026, 846억원 over 5 years) replacing about 90,000 devices installed in 2020. MOHW calls it the complement to face-to-face care, whose gaps are **nights, holidays and local staff shortages**. [V] — [MOHW (2026-04-01, see dating note in §2)](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1489949)
- **AI 안부 calls:** SK Telecom and the national 독거노인종합지원센터 piloted AI check-ins with 20,000 노인맞춤돌봄 users, with a plan to scale to about 500,000 (year not shown). [S] — [ITWorld](https://www.itworld.co.kr/article/3551089/). Seoul's 스마트 안부확인 had a 2025 budget of 7.94억원 for 28,000 at-risk households and became two-way in Oct 2025 (users can call the AI to request services). [S] — [서울시 미디어허브](https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2016457), [news.seoul.go.kr](https://news.seoul.go.kr/welfare/archives/tag/ai)
- **User voice on AI calls:** an expert says users experience AI check-in calls as "checking whether I'm dead or alive", dislike them, and that the calls lack real emotional interaction ("satisfying to the supplier"). The expert calls for more small neighbourhood spaces to talk and eat together, more visits by care aides, and 노노케어 jobs. [V, expert opinion] — [시사위크 via Daum 2026-05-08](https://v.daum.net/v/20260508142658426)
- **경로당 / 복지관 infrastructure (2025):** 69,687 경로당, 459 노인복지관, 1,163 노인교실. In-home care facilities total 19,146; visiting-bath providers fell to 4,135 (−13.7%). [V] — [2026 고령자 통계 p.31](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)
- **Actual reach in the 2023 survey:** only 7.3% of those living alone with functional limitation named 노인맞춤돌봄 as a care provider. Meal delivery is used by 6.5% of those living alone and 경로식당 by 13.2%. [V — 표 4-19, 3-17, 3-18]
- Policy direction: 고독사 prevention will extend to all socially isolated risk groups, with tailored services by life stage (youth, middle age, old age). A 고독사위기대응시스템 starts in 2026 and a social-isolation survey is planned for 2026. [V] — [세계일보 2025-11-27](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20251127517111). 의료·요양 돌봄통합지원법 took effect March 2026. [V] — [MOHW 2026-07-22](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1491373)

### Inferences
- **Middle-income gap:** 노인맞춤돌봄 requires 기초연금 eligibility (about the bottom 70%), so roughly 30% of older people living alone, those above the line, have no publicly funded in-person check-in or housework. Their only public option is the sensor-based 응급안전안심, which is income-free. Arithmetic for scale: about 2.48M households vs 550k 노인맞춤돌봄 users means about 22% reach, and about 300k sensor households means about 12% reach, even before the income cut-off.
- **Time gap:** 16–20 hours a month, delivered on weekdays, leaves nights, weekends, hospital days and post-discharge weeks uncovered. Users' own top improvement requests (more types, more hours, rehab) say the same.
- **Acceptance gap:** men and people still living independently say they "don't need help" or don't want to burden anyone. AI calls are perceived as death checks. Services framed as social or useful activity (shared meals, hobby, work) probably reach these groups better than services framed as care.

### Gaps
- No official 2025/2026 노인맞춤돌봄 user total or budget found in primary sources (blog claims of 47만 users and 5,894억원 are unverified).
- No 노인맞춤돌봄 user satisfaction or needs survey found (독거노인종합지원센터 or KIHASA evaluation). Searched without success.
- No sex breakdown of 노인맞춤돌봄 or 응급안전안심 users, so "men participate less" could not be quantified for these programmes. It is supported only indirectly (경로당/복지관 use, "don't need help" answers, 고독사).

## 4. Hobbies, leisure, community and male isolation

### Takeaway
Older people living alone use 경로당 (34.0%) and 복지관 (12.5%) **more** than couples do, but for meals and company rather than programmes. They are **less** willing to take up hobbies (32.0% vs 43.2%), travel or clubs. The main barrier is not cost but **not knowing what or how** (48.5% of non-participants living alone), then poor health (15.1%) and having nobody to go with (7.7%). Men, especially those who live alone after retirement, divorce or widowhood, are the most isolated and least reached group.

### Cited Findings
- **경로당 (표 9-27/9-28):** current use among those living alone **34.0%** (couples 21.7%; all 26.5%; rural 48.6%; 65–69 only 10.1%), 3.2 visits a week, 41.7% want to keep or start using it. Reasons for use, living alone: socialising 62.7% · **meals 25.1%** · health promotion 3.3% · lifelong learning 3.9% · hobby programmes 3.5%. [V]
- **노인복지관 (표 9-29):** current use among those living alone 12.5% (couples 8.4%), 24.2% want to use it. [V]
- **Desire for activities (those living alone, "very much" + "yes")**: hobbies 32.0% (couples 43.2%) · travel 36.9% · socialising 38.4% (couples 51.4%) · religion 28.5% · clubs 9.8% · learning 9.5% · volunteering 10.2% · politics 4.6%. [V — 표 9-19~26]
- **Reasons for not taking part in leisure in the past year** (표 9-6, non-participants living alone n=615): no money 12.7% · no time 9.2% · **don't know what to do 21.7% · don't know how 26.8%** · facility too far 6.7% · poor health 15.1% · nobody to go with 7.7%. Couples: no money 22.6%, nobody to go with 3.6%. [V; the 21.7/26.8 values are confirmed in the report text]
- Organised groups (all 65+): 경로당 use fell from 28.1% (2020) to 26.5% (2023), while membership of friendship groups rose from 44.1% to 54.2%. Club membership is 6.6% and volunteering 2.5%. [V — press release]
- TV and radio daily: 93.9% of those living alone (표 9-1) [I].
- **National leisure, 2025 Social Survey (65+):** weekend activities are video watching 80.3%, rest 78.4%, hobby 12.9%, sports 8.5%, tourism 10.5%, social activity 21.2%. Leisure satisfaction is 27.1%. Reasons for dissatisfaction: **financial burden 42.6%, health or stamina 39.3%**, lack of time 7.6%, nobody to go with 2.4%, lack of facilities 1.2%, lack of information 0.7%. Who they spend weekend leisure with: family 56.1%, alone 22.0% (men 20.3%, women 23.4%), friends 11.5%, clubs 9.7%. [V] — [2026 고령자 통계 pp.13–14](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)
  - Note the difference: in the Social Survey (all 65+, dissatisfaction) cost comes first. In the 2023 노인실태조사 (non-participants living alone) not knowing what or how comes first. Both are valid answers to different questions.
- **Male isolation:**
  - 고독사 is 81.7% male; men in their 50s–60s make up about 54% of all cases (2024). Officials attribute this to divorce or bereavement, job loss, and men not voicing difficulties. [V] — [세계일보 2025-11-27](https://www.segye.com/newsView/20251127517111)
  - Men's help networks shrank between 2015 and 2025 (−4.8%p when sick, −4.7%p when depressed), while women's grew. [V] — [2026 고령자 통계 p.12](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)
  - A KIHASA-linked analysis of 2017 노인실태조사 1-person households (men n=370, women n=1,770) found that for men, family ties did not predict friend or neighbour contact or 경로당/복지관 participation; for women, contact with family positively predicted meeting friends and neighbours. [S] — [koreascience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202021438867530.page)
  - A qualitative study of 12 men living alone describes community participation as starting from isolation caused by loss of role. [S] — [KIHASA 보건사회연구 journal](https://www.kihasa.re.kr/hswr/assets/pdf/1677/journal-46-2-135.pdf)
  - Field report: older men living alone find cooking burdensome and stay shut in at home. [S, 2022] — [머니투데이](https://www.mt.co.kr/society/2022/03/15/2022030813255773970)

### Inferences
- The leisure gap for older people living alone is mostly about **information and navigation** ("what can I do, how do I join") and **companionship**, more than money. That suggests demand for curated, low-barrier, accompanied entry into activities, such as a first visit with a peer.
- 경로당 functions as a food-and-company hub for women living alone. It doesn't attract younger-old people (10.1% use at 65–69) or men, who need different formats: purpose and role-based activities, work, practical skills.

### Gaps
- No 2023 노인실태조사 table splits 경로당/복지관 use or hobby interest by sex for those living alone. That needs the microdata.
- No Korean survey found that lists **which specific hobbies** older people living alone want (e.g., singing, exercise, crafts) vs do.

## 5. Voice of customer (adult children and older people living alone)

### Takeaway
Qualitative material is thin in primary news. What exists stresses being lonely **when sick**, holidays such as 어버이날 as loneliness peaks, AI calls felt as death checks, and adult children's interest in home cameras and sensors tempered by parents' privacy concerns. Most "adult child" content online is service how-to blogs, not testimony.

### Cited Findings
- 어버이날: older people who visit the welfare centre lonely and say they got there what they "never even got from my children". [V] — [시사위크 via Daum 2026-05-08](https://v.daum.net/v/20260508142658426)
- Expert (스스로랩): AI 안부 calls are seen as "checking whether I died" and are disliked. Small neighbourhood spaces to talk and eat together are needed. [V] — same source
- "When I'm sick I'm so lonely… I miss someone putting a hand on my forehead"; the need for a person who can be reached immediately in an emergency (e.g., recognising heart-attack symptoms and calling 119). [V, feature article] — [코메디닷컴 via Daum 2025-12-11](https://v.daum.net/v/20251211140312588)
- Adult children and home cameras: a Samsung SmartThings community thread on monitoring parents notes that parents "may feel bad" about cameras and that consent matters, especially with dementia. A vendor notes that camera viewing is a check-in, not an alarm, and may miss falls. [S] — [Samsung Community](https://r1.community.samsung.com/t5/smartthings/), [ispyconnect](https://www.ispyconnect.com/ko/solutions/elderly-parent-camera)
- A large number of 2026 blog guides target "50대 자녀" (adult children in their 50s) choosing services for parents living alone (eligibility, 7 things to check before applying). This suggests adult children are the active searchers and deciders. [S] — [tilnote](https://tilnote.io/pages/6a9e24e78a847deea1798eb3.md), [tndlrs](https://www.tndlrs.com/2026/10/elderly-customized-care-service-2026.html)
- Survey proxy for family concern: 53.4% of older people (2024) think caring for parents is a joint responsibility of family, government and society (+17.7%p in 10 years). Support from children or relatives as the main source of living costs fell to 13.1% (−15.5%p). [V] — [2026 고령자 통계 p.1](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)

### Inferences
- Adult children search and decide, and need to be reassured. The parent's acceptance depends on dignity. A service framed as surveillance (cameras, death-check calls) risks rejection. A service framed as a person, a relationship or practical help is accepted more readily.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic adult-child survey (e.g., "what worries you most about your parent living alone"). No posts from community sites (맘카페, 블라인드, 82cook) were reachable or retrieved. The VoC here is anecdotal.

## 6. Japan reference (brief)

### Takeaway
Japan is about a decade ahead. Older people living alone were about 6.7M in 2020 (men 15.0%, women 22.1% of the 65+ population) and are projected at about 9.0M by 2040 (men 20.8%, women 24.5%). The government treats single older people's isolation as a core 高齢社会白書 theme. Figures here are **snippet-only**, because www8.cao.go.jp was blocked by the session proxy.

### Cited Findings
- Older people living alone in Japan: 2020 = men about 2.31M, women about 4.41M (15.0% and 22.1% of the 65+ population). 2040 projection = men about 3.56M, women about 5.40M (20.8% and 24.5%). [S] — [総務省 資料](https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_content/000892181.pdf)
- The 2025 (令和7年版) 高齢社会白書 has a section stating that older people living alone are increasing. A secondary site quotes 2025 estimates of men 18.3% (about 2.9M) and women 25.4% (about 5.24M). Single-person households were 31.8% of households with a member 65+ in 2024 (about 8.79M). [S] — [幻冬舎GOLD](https://gentosha-go.com/articles/-/75707); white paper index [内閣府 w-2025](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2025/html/zenbun/index.html), 2026 edition fig. 1-1-9 [内閣府 w-2026 概要](https://www8.cao.go.jp/kourei/whitepaper/w-2026/html/gaiyou/s1_1.html)

### Inferences
- Japan's projected sex ratio (more women, with men's share rising fastest) mirrors Korea's direction. Japan's rising share of men living alone is a leading indicator for Korea's male-isolation problem.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: 内閣府 "高齢者の住宅と生活環境に関する調査" or 孤独・孤立 survey results for single older people; MHLW 見守り services; 配食 or 地域包括支援センター usage data. Any Japan service details (e.g., 郵便局 or utility-meter みまもり, 配食サービス) should be treated as background knowledge needing verification.
