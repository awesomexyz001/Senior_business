## 1. Segment size and trend (한부모가구 count, divorce share, divorces with minor children, 황혼이혼)

### Takeaway
There are about 1.49M 한부모가구 (2024), and the number is slowly shrinking (6.6% of households in 2023, down from 7.3% in 2019). About 84% of 한부모 heads with children aged 18 or under became single parents through divorce. New inflow is falling: 37,000 divorces involving minor children in 2025 (−4%, 42.5% of all divorces). 황혼이혼 (marriages of 30+ years) overtook divorces within 5 years of marriage for the first time. So the divorce segment is shifting toward older people without dependent children, while the divorced-with-children segment is shrinking but still large and persistent.

### Inferences
- Stock vs inflow: about 1.49M 한부모가구 overall. Applying the survey's 84% divorce share to the stock gives roughly 1.2M+ divorce-origin 한부모가구, but that is an extrapolation across different universes. Census 한부모가구 includes adult unmarried children, so the survey share should not be applied to it directly.
- About 37K new divorces with minor children per year means about 37K new custodial heads and about 37K new non-custodial parents each year. The non-custodial group is a large and under-served segment. No official stock count of it was found.
- Divorce demand is getting older. 황혼이혼 (no minor children) is now a bigger inflow than early-marriage divorce, so "divorced adult" needs increasingly overlap with middle-aged and older 1인가구 needs: housing split, pension division, loneliness.

## 2. Needs evidence — 한부모가족 실태조사 (2024 vs 2021), income, work, care, housing, emotions

### Takeaway
The 2024 survey shows a household that is employed but poor and cash-starved:
- Income is 294.6만원/month, 60.3% of the all-household average.
- Net assets are about a quarter of average, and debt more than doubled since 2021.
- 83.9% are economically active, but 30.8% are temporary or day workers.
- Two-thirds name cash support (생계비·양육비) as the #1 needed policy. Housing (12.9%) and childcare (6.3%) come next.
- Childcare problems are about finding a carer (66.7%) and lack of time (63.9%).
- Reported discrimination fell sharply, but social isolation (no one to ask for help) persists.

### Inferences
Ranked needs (my synthesis, weighted by survey share and severity):
1. **Cash and income adequacy.** The 66.9% #1-policy answer, the 60% income ratio, and debt doubling since 2021 all point here. 모자가구 at about 51% of average income are the core of the poverty.
2. **Child support (양육비) enforcement** (see §3). This is structurally tied to need 1.
3. **Housing.** Only 23.6% own their home, half of debt is housing-related, and housing is the #2 policy need.
4. **Childcare and time.** Finding a carer is hard (66.7%), and children are left alone 2+ hours a day. The fixed or short hours of public 아이돌봄 do not match precarious, small-firm, long-hour jobs.
5. **Job quality and work–care balance.** Activity is high but quality is low (temporary work, firms under 10 staff). The need is better jobs with flexibility, more than "any job."
6. **Emotional support and isolation.** Depression is linked to discrimination and low income, and many have no one to turn to.
7. **Stigma.** It is declining sharply in institutions (21.1%→5.6%) but remains a mediator of depression.
- Signal for providers: needs 1–3 are dominated by public transfers. Market-addressable gaps are mostly in needs 4–6 (flexible or emergency childcare, household help, peer support, and counseling at low cost).

## 3. 양육비 collection — 실태조사 receipt rates, 양육비이행관리원, 2025-07 선지급제 results

### Takeaway
Seven in ten divorced or unmarried 한부모 have never received child support (71.3% in 2024, barely changed from 73.1% in 2018). Having a legal 양육비 채권 is decisive: 80.1% receive with one vs 2.6% without, yet only 33.3% hold one. The state 선지급제 (from 2025-07-01) reached about 8.5K households and about 253억원 by 2026-09. However, recovery from debtor parents is only about 14.7% by amount, and the income test was abolished from 2026-10-29.

### Inferences
- 양육비 is the single sharpest unmet need, and the problem is concentrated in parents without a legal 채권 (two-thirds). That points to front-end needs: legal help to obtain a 채권 at divorce, including mediation, 이행권고, and court fees.
- With 선지급 now income-unrestricted, applications should jump in late 2026 and 2027. The recovery bottleneck (14.7%) is a fiscal-sustainability risk and may drive tougher sanctions, such as license suspension, travel bans, and name disclosure.
- Emotional dimension: chasing an ex for money prolongs conflict. Custodial parents need an intermediary so they do not have to deal with the ex directly.

## 4. Mothers vs fathers (모자 vs 부자가정) and non-custodial divorced adults

### Takeaway
Mothers face sharper income poverty (250.6만 vs 325.3만원). Research and commentary describe fathers as facing a sudden jump in housework and caregiving load and a reluctance to seek help. Korean data that cleanly separates father-specific isolation was not found. Non-custodial parents' main touchpoint is the court-run 면접교섭센터. Its service is thin: 2 visits a month for 6 months, 1 hour each.

### Cited Findings
- 모자가구 income 250.6만원 (about 51% of the all-household average) vs 부자가구 325.3만원 (2024 survey). [snippet-only] — [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202503302023025)
- Fathers: as head of a 부자가족 they see sharply increased childcare and housework and the burden of becoming the primary carer. Mothers more often have to start work for income, while fathers' hardship centers on care and housework. ### Inferences

- Non-custodial divorced adults (mostly fathers) have needs that are almost unserved: keeping a relationship with their children after the 6-month court window, payment-plan help, housing as a newly single 1인가구, and emotional recovery. ## 5. Remarriage and stepfamily (재혼가정), dating, and post-divorce emotional recovery

### Takeaway
Stepfamily legal needs center on children's 성·본 and the legal tie to the stepparent. The routes are a court-approved 성본변경 (judged on child welfare and the child's own view) or 친양자 입양, which ends the legal tie to the biological parent and normally requires that parent's consent. That consent is a frequent conflict point. Dating-market voice-of-customer shows a need for verified, divorced-only matching, disclosure etiquette, safety, and fair pricing. No public stepfamily-support program was found.

## 7. Japan reference (brief)

### Takeaway
Japan's 養育費 receipt is also low: 28.1% of 母子 and 8.7% of 父子 currently receive (FY2021 survey). The state co-funds municipal 養育費保証 contracts (国 1/2, local 1/2; 249 municipalities in FY2023). The 2026-04-01 civil-code reform introduced optional joint custody, a default 法定養育費 of ¥20,000 per child per month, and a statutory lien (先取特権) that lets parents enforce unpaid support without a court title.

### Cited Findings
- 令和3年度全国ひとり親世帯等調査 [snippet-only] — [こども家庭庁 令和7年1月資料](https://www.cfa.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/0a870592-1814-4b21-bf56-16f06080c594/7cf5dd42/20250107_policies_hitori-oya_73.pdf)
  - 養育費 agreement: 母子 46.7%, 父子 28.3%.
  - Currently receiving, among those with an agreement: 母子 57.7%, 父子 25.9%.
  - Currently receiving, overall: 母子 28.1%, 父子 8.7%.
  - Targets for 2031: 40% overall receipt and 70% where an agreement exists, to be reviewed after the 2026 survey.
- 離婚前後家庭支援事業 (養育費確保等支援パッケージ) [snippet-only] — [こども家庭庁](https://www.cfa.go.jp/assets/contents/node/basic_page/field_ref_resources/0a870592-1814-4b21-bf56-16f06080c594/7cf5dd42/20250107_policies_hitori-oya_73.pdf)
  - Subsidizes the cost of signing 養育費保証 contracts with guarantee companies.
  - Subsidy rate is 国 1/2 and local 1/2.
  - 249 municipalities took part in FY2023.
- 2026-04-01 reform [snippet-only] — [日本経済新聞](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUA310C20R31C25A0000000/); [elaws.jp解説](https://elaws.jp/article/joint-custody-civil-code-reform-2026); [office-tree解説](https://office-tree.jp/blog/divorce/joint-custody-2026/)

  - The 先取特権 allows seizure without a court title, capped at about ¥80,000 per month. The cap was reported at the draft-ordinance stage, so verify it.

### Inferences
- Korea's state 선지급 (advance payment plus state recovery) and Japan's privately guaranteed, municipally subsidized model are two contrasting designs. Japan's statutory default amount and lien are ideas Korea's 국회 debate on stronger enforcement may borrow.
