## 1. Current and projected senior population (65+, 75+, 85+), super-aged entry, single-person senior households

### Takeaway
Korea became a super-aged society (65+ ≥ 20%) on 2024-12-23 (10.24M, 20.0% of registered residents). As of 2026 the 65+ population is 11.125M (21.6%). Statistics Korea's 2022-based projection (released Dec 2023) has the share at about 25.3% in 2030, 29.9% in 2035 (15.2M) and above 30% in 2036. Single-person senior households are about 2.48M in 2026 (38.0% of senior-headed households). They are projected to reach about 3.35M by 2037 and 4.96M by 2052.

### Cited Findings
**Super-aged entry (Dec 2024)**
- On 2024-12-23 the 65+ registered population was 10,244,550, which is 20.0% of 51,221,286 registered residents (행정안전부). The share was 17.83% for men and 22.15% for women, and 전남 had the highest share. Korea moved from aging society (2000) to aged (2017) to super-aged (2024) in 7 years; Japan took 12 years for the last step. Experts had expected entry in 2025. — [Newsis 2024-12-24](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20241224_0003009249); [Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20241224_0003009282); [한경 Business](https://magazine.hankyung.com/business/article/202412294183b)
- Note: this uses 주민등록 (resident registration). Statistics Korea's estimated-population basis gives 20.3% for 2025. The two series differ slightly. — [2025 고령자 통계 (kostat)](https://kostat.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10301010000&bid=10820&tag=&act=view&list_no=438832&ref_bid=); [브라보마이라이프](https://bravo.etoday.co.kr/view/atc_view/17445)

**Current level (latest official, 2026 고령자 통계, released 2026-10-01 by 국가데이터처, formerly 통계청)**

- 2023 노인실태조사 (individual-level, n=10,078): 55.2% of older adults live as couples, 32.8% alone, 10.3% with children. Living alone rose by about 13%p versus the prior survey (19.8%, as reported). People living alone show worse health, depression and daily-living difficulties. — [보건복지부 2023 노인실태조사 보도자료](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1483352&tag=&nPage=1); [아시아타임즈](https://www.asiatime.co.kr/article/20241016500265)

These are different bases and should not be compared directly. The "+13%p" jump in 노인실태조사 is as reported by the media; verify against the original.

### Inferences
- 65+ grows by about 4.1M between 2026 (11.1M) and 2035 (about 15.2M), roughly 450K net per year. This is driven by the 1st boomers (1955–63) aging into 70s and the 2nd boomers (1964–74) entering 65+ from 2029.
- 85+ nearly doubles between 2023 and 2033. Care, dementia and family-caregiver ("adult child as buyer") services face the fastest volume growth.
- Single-person senior households are at about 2.5M and rising toward about 3.4M (2037). This is a large, addressable base for safety, check-in, companionship and daily-life admin services.

### Gaps
- Official counts of 75+ (후기고령자) for 2026, 2030 and 2035 were not retrieved. KOSIS tables were not reachable, and the search did not surface 75+ counts. Pull from KOSIS 장래인구추계 연령별 table.
- A newer 장래인구추계 (e.g., 2024-based) was not found as of this search. The 2022-based (Dec 2023) edition appears to be the latest national projection.

## 2. Cohort differences: 1차 베이비부머 (1955–63) vs 2차 베이비부머 (1964–74) vs 75+

### Takeaway
2nd boomers (9.54M) are larger, more educated and earn more than 1st boomers (7.05M). They hold more financial assets and more debt, and have longer 국민연금 contribution histories. 1st boomers are real-estate heavy and more dependent on public transfers. Late-old (70s+/75+) seniors have markedly lower income and digital capability. The most digitally capable, highest-income target is the 50s–60s (2nd boomers plus younger 1st boomers).

### Cited Findings
**Size and timing**
- 2nd boomers (1964–74) number 9.54M and enter the statutory retirement age (60) over 11 years starting in 2024. 1st boomers number 7.05M. Bank of Korea estimates the 2nd-boomer retirement lowers annual GDP growth by about 0.38%p on average over 2024–2034. — [한국경제 2024-07](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202407013115i); [Daum/news](https://v.daum.net/v/20250929120317878)

**Income and assets (KIHASA analysis, reported 2025-03)**
- 2022 annual wage income was 21.04M won for 1st boomers and 34.66M won for 2nd boomers. Public transfer income (기초연금 etc.) was 7.51M won for 1st boomers versus 2.21M won for 2nd, more than 3x higher for the 1st cohort. — [헤럴드경제](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10431387); [네이트 뉴스](https://news.nate.com/view/20250302n09397)
- 1st boomers hold assets concentrated in their home and other real estate. 2nd boomers have a higher financial-asset share (financial assets 139.27M won) and sharply higher debt (107.88M won). — [헤럴드경제](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10431387)
- CONFLICT: a separate column claims 2nd boomers also hold more than 80% of assets in real estate. — [시대 칼럼](https://www.sidae.com/article/2024072616253474893). KIHASA's "higher financial share" is relative, so both can hold. Treat the column as lower-quality.
- 2023 50s household real income was 71.2M won, 27.9% above 50s households in 2013 (55.64M won). — [한국경제 2025-02](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2025021713241)

**Pension (국민연금)**
- 1st boomers were already in their 30s–40s when 국민연금 started in 1988. They have shorter contribution periods and more households with low pension receipts. 2nd boomers have longer contribution histories. — [헤럴드경제](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10431387)
- 65+ pension receipt rate (any public or private pension) was 91.2% in 2024, with an average of 737,000 won per month. — [2026 고령자 통계 PDF](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4); [아시아경제](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026100113111133822)
- CONFLICT (vintage): the 2025 고령자 통계 reported an average monthly receipt of 695,000 won (prior reference year). The difference is a year-of-reference effect, not a true discrepancy. — [뉴스서천/topictree summaries](https://topictree.co.kr/economy/korea-2025-elderly-retirement-cash-income/)

**All-65+ wealth and income**
- Average net assets of 65+-headed households were 492.79M won in 2025 (2026 고령자 통계), up from 465.94M won in 2024 (2025 고령자 통계, +10.54M won). About 80% is real estate and about 14% savings, so liquidity is low. — [뉴스서천](https://www.newssc.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=60740); [2026 고령자 통계](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=10820&list_no=447294&seq=4)
- 74.7% of seniors fund their own living costs themselves or with a spouse. Main sources are labor or business income (48.2%) and pensions or severance (38.3%) (2025 고령자 통계). — [브라보마이라이프](https://bravo.etoday.co.kr/view/atc_view/17445)
- 65+ employed persons: 4,147,000 (2026 고령자 통계). — [데일리팝](http://www.dailypop.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=103457)
- Home-pension (주택연금) uptake is only about 2% despite real-estate-heavy balance sheets. — [한국데이터경제신문](https://www.dataeconomy.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=43217)
- 2023 노인실태조사: annual household income of older-adult households was 34.69M won (+4.42M vs 2020), personal income 21.64M won, financial assets 49.12M won, and real estate (including jeonse deposits) 318.17M won. — [보건복지부](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503010100&bid=0027&act=view&list_no=1483352&tag=&nPage=1); [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202410162131055)

**Age gradient within 65+ (young-old vs late-old)**
- Personal annual income is 30.71M won at age 65–69 versus 20.26M won at 70–74, a gap of more than 10M won (2023 노인실태조사). — [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202410162131055); [서울경제](https://www.sedaily.com/article/13965210)
- Seniors with high-school education or higher rose from 17.2% (2008) to 34.3% (2020) to 38.2% (2023). The media attribute this to boomers entering old age, creating a "신노년층". About 24% say they will spend their assets rather than leave them as inheritance. — [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202410162131055); [Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/1Ywg5lSgh8)
- Digital capability by age: NIA 2025 digital informatization level is 79.7% for people in their 60s and 53.6% for 70s+ (see section 4).

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay: the 2nd boomers (now aged 52–62) have the highest earned income, the most financial assets and the most digital fluency. They also carry high debt and face retirement-income cliffs before pension age (65 for these cohorts). Products that help them through that transition (retirement income planning, re-employment, health) or that serve their aging parents (75+/85+) are a strong double-target.
- The 75+ group has low capability and low liquidity. They are more likely to be the user than the payer; the payer is the adult child or government (장기요양 / voucher). B2B2C or family-paid models fit better than direct subscriptions.

### Gaps
- No direct survey data was found on willingness to pay for digital subscriptions by cohort (1st vs 2nd boomer vs 75+).
- Cohort-specific education breakdowns (e.g., college share of 2nd boomers) were not retrieved.
- Cohort-specific 국민연금 average benefit amounts were not retrieved. The NPS 통계연보 would have these.

## 3. Senior market (고령친화산업) size and growth, total and by sub-industry

### Takeaway
KHIDI measures the 2024 고령친화산업 at about 81.8 trillion won. That is down 4.0% year on year because the finance segment shrank; manufacturing grew 6.9%. Long-run growth is about 6.6% CAGR (2010–2024). 2030 forecasts vary widely, from 168 to 241 trillion won, and are much higher than the trend implies. Treat them with caution.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 total was 81.7606 trillion won, −4.0% year on year, driven by a sharp drop in elderly-friendly financial services (attributed to shrinking senior asset bases, the rate environment and distrust of products). Manufacturing was 27.5895 trillion won (+6.9%) (KHIDI, "2024년 고령친화산업 제조·서비스업 실태조사"). — [KIDD 의료기기뉴스](https://kidd.co.kr/news/246234); [한국IT산업뉴스](https://www.koreaiin.com/news/909104)
- Sub-segments for 2024: 의약품 13.905 trillion won (largest manufacturing item), 요양 서비스 15.3266 trillion won, 여가 서비스 4.8031 trillion won. — [KIDD](https://kidd.co.kr/news/246234); [아주경제 2026-09-28](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20260928153838236)
- CONFLICT (growth direction): Ajunews (2026-09) describes the market as an "80조 고령친화시장" with 요양 at 15.33 trillion won, "+11.7% 급증". KIDD and 한국IT산업뉴스 report the total fell 4.0%. These can both hold (요양 up, total down because of finance) but the framing differs. An older 보건뉴스 headline claims "73조원… 연평균 두자릿수 성장", which conflicts with KIRI's 6.6% CAGR. — [아주경제](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20260928153838236); [보건뉴스](http://m.bokuennews.com/m/m_article.html?no=245776); [KIRI 리포트](https://www.kiri.or.kr/report/downloadFile.do?docId=868939)
- KIRI: the market was 81.8 trillion won in 2024, with 6.6% CAGR over 2010–2024. — [KIRI 리포트 (에이지테크)](https://www.kiri.or.kr/report/downloadFile.do?docId=868939)
- Historical series: 2021 was 72.3 trillion won (김택식 외 2022; 보건복지부·KHIDI 2022). 2022 was 82.7 trillion won, with KHIDI forecasting 106.1 trillion won in 2026 (5.8% CAGR), as cited by 하나금융경영연구소 (2025). — [KHISS 2023 실태조사](https://www.khiss.go.kr/board/view?menuId=MENU00308&linkId=176393); [KIRI](https://www.kiri.or.kr/report/downloadFile.do?docId=868939)
- 2030 forecasts conflict: (a) 73 trillion won (2020) to 241 trillion won (2030), attributed to 하나금융연구소/KHIDI in news; (b) 72 trillion won (2020) to 168 trillion won (2030), from a market-research report summary. — [고령친화산업 news summary](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20260928153838236); [researchcompany.co.kr](https://researchcompany.co.kr/goods/view_contents?no=946&zoom=1&view_preload=1)
- KHIDI expects finance, cosmetics, long-term care, pharmaceuticals and food to lead growth. — [KHIDI 보도](https://www.khidi.or.kr/board/view?linkId=48728796&menuId=MENU02200)
- Food: elderly-friendly food was above 3 trillion won (around 2022–2023) and is forecast at 5 trillion won by 2030 (aT/KHIDI). The article is older (about 3.4 years). — [식품음료신문](https://www.thinkfood.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=98039)
- Cosmetics: KIET-type methodology applies the senior consumption share of production (1.5%), giving about 1.1 trillion won. Critics say this undercounts. — [THE K BEAUTY SCIENCE](https://www.thekbs.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=12554)
- Structure: of 1,096 elderly-goods manufacturers, 82.5% have fewer than 10 employees and only 21.0% have R&D. 2023 elderly-goods (용품) manufacturing sales were 4.2083 trillion won, −5.4% year on year. — [KIDD](https://kidd.co.kr/news/246234); [THE Biz](http://www.the-biz.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=653697)
- 65+ household consumption grew from 1.47M to 1.82M won per month (2020 to 2024, +5% CAGR versus 4% for all households). Entertainment and culture spending more than doubled. Housing and health still carry the largest shares (하나금융연구소, "통계로 본 시니어의 소비 DNA 재편", 2025-03-24). — [하나금융그룹 웹진](https://www.hana1qm.com/webzine/web/2599/pdsView.do)

### Inferences
- Implied 2023 total ≈ 81.76 / 0.96 ≈ 85.2 trillion won (derived, not reported). So the market went from 72.3 (2021) to 82.7 (2022) to about 85 (2023) to 81.8 (2024): volatile, and moved by the finance segment.
- The 241 trillion won figure implies about 20% CAGR from 2024, roughly 3x the historical 6.6%. The 168 trillion won figure implies about 12.7%. A trend-based 2030 estimate at 6–7% CAGR is about 120 trillion won (derived). The 168–241 trillion won headline figures should be presented as optimistic.
- For software startups: KHIDI's 고령친화산업 is dominated by drugs, care services and finance. "Digital/agetech services" are not a separately measured segment, so the bottom-up TAM for a software product must be built from population × adoption × ARPU, not from these totals.

### Gaps
- 2024 figures for 의료기기, 식품, 화장품, 주거 and 금융 (absolute values) were not retrieved from the KHIDI report text.
- The KIET 산업연구원 and 삼성/LG경제연구원 senior-market forecasts (e.g., the older 2020 "125 trillion won" type projections) were not located in recent form. Older KHIDI forecasts (125 trillion won for 2020) overshot reality (about 72 trillion won).

## 4. Digital divide (NIA 디지털정보격차 실태조사) and app usage of 60s/70s

### Takeaway
Access is nearly universal; capability is the bottleneck. The 2025 NIA survey puts the 고령층 (55+) digital informatization level at 71.8% of the general population (access about 95, capability about 56, utilization about 76). There is a cliff between people in their 60s (79.7%) and 70s+ (53.6%). On apps, Coupang is the #1 shopping app among 60+, YouTube leads time-spent growth, and Temu ranks #2 among older shoppers.

### Cited Findings
**NIA / 과기정통부 디지털정보격차 실태조사**
- 2025 survey (in-person interviews, Oct–Dec 2025): 고령층 level 71.8% (general population = 100), the lowest of all vulnerable groups (농어민 80.6%; all vulnerable groups 77.9%, +0.4%p). Sub-indices are about 95.4 access, 56.2 capability and 75.6 utilization. — [FairAI 2025 디지털정보격차 실태조사 보고](https://m.fairai.or.kr/research/reports/423); [디지털포용뉴스](https://www.dginclusion.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=1537)
- By age within 고령층: 60s 79.7%, 70s+ 53.6%. By income: under 1M won per month household 49.8%, 1–1.99M won 48.2%. — [디지털포용뉴스](https://www.dginclusion.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=1537)
- Trend: 고령층 at 69.1% (2021) and 71.4% (2024), so gains are slow. — [디지털포용뉴스](https://www.dginclusion.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=635); [전자신문 2025-03-27 (2024 survey)](https://www.etnews.com/20250327000412)
- CONFLICT: some articles cite 71.4% as the latest, which is the 2024 figure. 2025 = 71.8%. Sub-index decimals vary slightly across reports. — [경인매일](https://www.kmaeil.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=651038)

**2023 노인실태조사 (65+, MOHW/KIHASA)**

### Inferences
- The digital ability cliff is at about age 70. People in their 60s are near-mainstream (about 80% of the general level); 70s+ are at about 54%. A self-serve app should target 55–69. A 75+ product needs assisted onboarding, voice or very simple UI, or a family/caregiver-operated model.
- Capability (56) rather than access (95) is the gap. Seniors own smartphones but struggle with complex tasks (finance, kiosks, e-commerce at about 20%). That leaves room for "do-it-for-me" or concierge-style digital services.
- Coupang and YouTube dominance suggests senior acquisition channels are YouTube content and marketplace listings, plus 당근 for local services.

## 5. Key consumption trends among 5060 / 액티브 시니어 (신중년, 디지털 시니어, 오팔세대)

### Takeaway
The "new old" (신노년층) cohort is better educated, wealthier and more digitally self-reliant. Spending is shifting from essentials to leisure, culture, learning and convenience (delivery, online shopping), and attitudes are moving from bequest toward "spend it myself". Asset-rich but cash-poor balance sheets and pension gaps keep money anxiety, tax-saving and re-employment as central concerns.

### Inferences
- Promising startup wedges where these trends meet the data above:
  - 50s–60s (2nd boomers): retirement cash-flow, tax and pension optimization; re-employment and skills; leisure, learning and community.
  - 75+/single-person households (about 2.5M rising to 3.4M by 2037): safety, check-ins, admin help. Usually paid by adult children or local government.
  - Digital legacy and end-of-life planning: an early-stage category in Korea.
- The "spend-it-myself" attitude (about 24%) plus rising culture spending support paid experiences and learning for the young-old. Low liquidity (80% real estate) argues for low-ticket or subscription pricing rather than large upfront fees.
